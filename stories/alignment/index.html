Half a star

In 2026 Alignment Healthcare signed up more seniors than ever. From its July high, its stock lost more than two-thirds of its value anyway. Then, in October, Medicare took away half a star.

That half star wasn't the biggest blow of the year. It may be the one that lasts longest. Scroll to see why.

From Alignment's SEC filings, Medicare's own fact sheets and daily share prices through …. Sources and checks are at the end.

Every fall, Medicare grades the private insurers that run Medicare Advantage plans, from one to five stars.

This was the grade for 2026 for Alignment's biggest plan, an HMO in California: four stars. About three in four of Alignment's members are in it.

On October 8, Medicare released the grades for 2027. The California plan got three and a half.

Alignment says that's mostly because the bar went up across the industry, and because it slipped on a few measures that count triple. It plans to appeal and to sue.

The grade landed on a company that, by one measure, was having its best year ever. Each dot is 1,000 members.

So why single out half a star? Because the other bad news is about this year. The half star is about 2028.

Now the stars. For plans rated four stars or more, Medicare raises the benchmark it uses to set their pay by 5 percentage points, or 10 in some counties.

Not all of that reaches the insurer's revenue, and Alignment hasn't said what the bonus is worth to it. But set even part of five dollars next to … and you can see the problem.

Stars also set how much of a plan's savings Medicare hands back as a rebate, but four stars and three and a half get the same share, 65%. For Alignment, the bonus is the whole loss.

It's a new problem for Alignment. Its California HMO was rated four stars or better for nine years in a row, 2018 to 2026.

The 2027 grade breaks the streak.

In January, Alignment told investors that every one of its members was in a plan rated four stars or more. The industry figure was ….

For 2027 the industry is at …. Alignment, with three in four members in a 3.5-star plan, is at a quarter at most.

The strangest part is the timing. The 2027 grades mostly measure care given in 2025. They set the bonus paid in 2028. Alignment says its revenue in 2026 and 2027 won't be touched.

So the grade looks back two years and bites two years ahead. The next grades, due in the fall of 2027, can only fix 2029. For 2028, Alignment's hope is its appeals and its lawsuit.

How this was made

pipeline/build_alignment.py downloads every source listed below and writes the three files in data/ that this page reads. It stops if any check fails, and it logs every download and check in data/build_log.txt.

Membership comes from the first highlights paragraph of each quarterly earnings release, which Alignment files with the SEC as Exhibit 99.1 to a Form 8-K. The revenue printed in each release is checked against Alignment's machine-readable SEC filings (XBRL), and all … quarters match. The $100 split uses the XBRL income statement for January to September 2026: care is operating expenses minus selling, general and administrative costs and depreciation, and the four parts add back up to revenue. Operating profit is before interest and taxes; after them, Alignment kept … of every $100.

Share prices are daily closes from Yahoo Finance. Alignment has never split its stock. Two closes reported in the press, June 9 and September 15, 2026, match. Market value is the closing price times shares outstanding on the cover of the latest 10-Q filed before that day (… and …), so it is approximate. The drops are close-to-close changes: the day before the news to the day after it, or two days for the conference.

The reasons behind each drop come from Alignment's filings where they exist: the lawsuit from its third-quarter 10-Q, the half star from its October 8 8-K, the lower forecast from its third-quarter release. Two come from the press only: what was said on the second-quarter call (StockStory) and at the September 15 conference (Orange County Business Journal). The page doesn't claim any one piece of news caused any one drop. Other things moved the stock on those days too.

    What is left out

    • A dollar value for the lost bonus. Alignment hasn't given one, and it depends on its bids, its counties (some get a double bonus) and its deals with doctors, which Alignment says will absorb part of the hit. Five points of benchmark is not five dollars of revenue, so the dashed squares show the size of the rule, not a forecast.
    • Star grades for the California HMO before 2025. The deck says only “4 stars or greater”, so the earlier stars are drawn as full stars without saying whether any was a 4.5.
    • Every other reason the stock moved. The five days marked are the biggest drops after the July high with a reported cause; the rest of the decline is grouped as “all other days”.
