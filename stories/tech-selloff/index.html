How this was made

The index line uses daily closing levels of the Nasdaq-100 (ticker ^NDX). Drawdown on each day is close ÷ highest close so far − 1. Because the series starts on …, “highest so far” means the highest close within this window. An earlier, higher close would make every drawdown here deeper.

Market value per company is the closing price times shares outstanding. Shares come from the cover page of each company's most recent 10-Q, 10-K, 20-F or 40-F, where every share class is tagged as dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding. Classes are summed and priced at the listed share, so all of Alphabet's classes are valued at the Class A price. For foreign companies that trade in New York as depositary shares, the count is converted at the ratio printed on the cover. The same share count is used for every day, so buybacks or new issuance between the peak and the filing are ignored. This is total company value, not the index's own modified weighting, so it moves a little differently from the index: the members' combined value fell … from peak to latest, against … for the index. In the square chart each company's loss is rounded to whole squares, so small losses can show as none; the totals in the labels are exact.
