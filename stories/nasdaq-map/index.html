How this was made

Market value for each company on each day is its closing price times its shares outstanding. These numbers are shared with the companion story, How much the tech selloff has taken back, so the two pages agree. Members are the Nasdaq-100 as it stood on the peak day, …. …

I also fetched every member's daily closes separately from stockanalysis.com (data from S&P Global Market Intelligence) as a check. All … closes that both sources cover agree to the cent.

This is total company value, not the float-adjusted, capped weight the index itself uses. That matters for companies with a small public float. SpaceX, for example, gets land for its whole valuation even though most of its shares don't trade. Alphabet appears once, with both share classes added together.

The peak is …, the Nasdaq-100's record close in the daily index data the companion story uses, and the same day news reports give.

The map is the continental 48 states plus DC from the U.S. Census Bureau's cartographic boundary files, as packaged in us-atlas 3.0.1, drawn in an Albers equal-area projection, so equal areas on screen are equal areas of land. The country is cut into patches by repeatedly splitting the list of companies into two halves of nearly equal value and cutting the map along its longer side so each side gets exactly its share of the area. A patch's area matches its company's share to within a millionth before the outlines are smoothed for drawing. State comparisons use the same projected areas.

In the drain view each patch keeps its peak outline and fills from the bottom to the fraction of its peak value it still has. A company worth more than at the peak is drawn full, so the drawn total there can slightly overstate what's left; the numbers in the text use the exact sums. In the shrink view the whole outline is scaled so its area matches the day's total, and the patches are redrawn inside it at that day's values.

All figures are computed by pipeline/build_nasdaq_map.py from the companion story's constituent_daily.csv and the map files in data/raw/.

Sources

  1. Members: Invesco QQQ Trust holdings in its SEC N-PORT filing, adjusted for Nasdaq-100 changes through the peak, as built by the companion story's pipeline.
  2. Shares outstanding: each company's latest SEC 10-Q, 10-K, 20-F or 40-F cover page.
  3. Daily closes: Yahoo Finance; cross-checked against stockanalysis.com historical price pages.
  4. Index peak date context: The Private Banker, "Nasdaq 100 Falls Into Correction as Intel, Nvidia and Reddit Lead Tech Rout," Oct 30, 2026; Newsquawk headline, Oct 30, 2026.
  5. State boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary files via us-atlas 3.0.1 (states-albers-10m).
