How this was made

Profit here is net income attributable to the company's shareholders (the XBRL tag us-gaap:NetIncomeLoss), as reported in each company's own 10-K and 10-Q filings with the SEC. For each company I take the latest twelve months it has reported: the last full fiscal year, plus this year to date, minus the same period a year earlier. Where the latest report is the annual report, that year is used as is. Each total is checked against the sum of the four quarters the filings give, and the build stops if they disagree.

Time to make a million is $1,000,000 ÷ (net income ÷ seconds in those twelve months). The twelve months are counted by their real length, since some fiscal years run 52 or 53 weeks. Net income is earned unevenly, of course: this is the average pace, as if it ticked in steadily day and night.

Each cradle strikes once per $10,000 of profit. Every cradle runs in real time, so the time between strikes on screen is the number in its label. The balls swing like a pendulum, and fast ones are drawn with motion blur. The cradles are rendered in 3D with three.js (MIT license).

Fiscal years differ, so the twelve-month windows don't all end on the same date. The table at the end gives each company's window. Data was pulled from SEC EDGAR on …, using filings made by that day. All figures are computed by pipeline/build_cradles.py, which writes the CSV this page reads. Nothing is typed in by hand.

Ten years ago: the same calculation for the twelve months ending ten years before each company's latest window, using figures as first reported at the time (later restatements, such as new accounting rules applied to old years, are ignored so that annual and quarterly figures match). Market value, used only for the two companies compared in the middle of the story, is shares outstanding from the cover of each company's latest 10-Q times its last closing price before the data was pulled.

Sources

  1. Net income: SEC EDGAR XBRL company facts API (data.sec.gov), from each company's 10-K and 10-Q filings. Accession numbers for every figure are in the table below, and the full arithmetic is in build_log.txt.
  2. Closing prices for the market-value comparison: Yahoo Finance daily chart data.
  3. Company identifiers: SEC's ticker-to-CIK list.
  4. S&P 500 membership for the picker: SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY) daily holdings, State Street. Company names as listed on Wikipedia's List of S&P 500 companies. Net income for each from the SEC's XBRL company-concept API; the arithmetic is in sp500_log.txt and the figures in sp500.csv.
