Market Visualization

Who ate whom

Thirty years of American media and telecom, played out as a fish tank. Every fish is a company, drawn as big as the stock market said it was worth at the time. When one company buys another, a fish gets eaten. When a company splits up, it pops like a bubble and the pieces swim off on their own.

Fish that keep swallowing big rivals turn into predators. Small upstarts start as guppies and grow up. It plays by itself and stops at the biggest moments.

How this was made

Each fish's body area is proportional to the company's value on that date, in dollars of the day, not adjusted for inflation. Between two dated values the size is eased smoothly from one to the next. Fish smaller than a few pixels are drawn at a minimum size so they stay visible.

Mergers and splits are placed on the date the deal closed, not the date it was announced. Every event below lists the article it was checked against: the build script fetches each source and refuses to run if the quoted passage isn't in it.

All figures are computed by pipeline/build_fish_tank.py, which writes the files this page reads. Nothing on the page is typed in by hand.
